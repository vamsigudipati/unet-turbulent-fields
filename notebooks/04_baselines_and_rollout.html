Stage 4 — Baselines and rollout¶

Vamsi Gudipati

Stages 1 and 3 train the same U-Net (four levels, 16 base filters, 1,940,817 parameters) on the same 5,777 channel-flow planes of the streamwise velocity u. Each compares its U-Net with one simple reference on its own terms: bilinear interpolation in Stage 1, persistence (the next field equals the current one) in Stage 3. Here I measure both comparisons more widely with the models those stages trained and saved; nothing is retrained.

  • A. Super-resolution against classical upscaling. On the 868 test planes of Stage 1 (the last 15 % in its order of snapshot and plane), the 52×96 coarse field (area average, factor 4) is brought back to 208×384 by nearest-neighbour, bilinear (Stage 1's own input) and bicubic interpolation, and by the U-Nets: Stage 1's main model (one training), the three reduced-budget factor-4 models of its experiments (seeds 0, 1, 2) and, when present, the pretrained model trained elsewhere. Error per field and the streamwise energy spectrum, so that the U-Net's gain over a classical upscale is measured and located in scale.
  • B. Temporal evolution against persistence. With Stage 3's three main models (seeds 0, 1, 2) and, when present, the pretrained model trained elsewhere: the one-step error on the 857 validation pairs, then autoregressive rollouts (each prediction fed back as the next input) as far as the data allow, with the error against the horizon for the U-Net and for persistence and the horizon at which the U-Net stops beating persistence.

Every error is computed on the 201 physical rows (the 7 padded rows are cropped) in units normalised by the training standard deviation of u, unless the column says otherwise.

A. Super-resolution against classical upscaling¶

The test planes, the coarse field and the bilinear input are rebuilt exactly as in Stage 1: planes sorted by snapshot and plane index, split 70 / 15 / 15 % in that order, edge padding to 208 rows, tf.image.resize with area down to 52×96 and bilinear back up. Nearest-neighbour and bicubic use the same tf.image.resize with nearest and bicubic. Each U-Net receives the bilinear input normalised with its own saved mean and standard deviation (all equal to Stage 1's training statistics) and its output is mapped back.

The energy spectrum is the streamwise one: for each row y of a plane, the fluctuation u′ = u − mean over x is Fourier-transformed along x (the channel is periodic in x) and |û(k)|² is averaged over the rows and the test planes; k is the wavenumber index (k cycles over the 384-point length). The coarse grid has 96 points along x, so it carries wavenumbers up to k = 48; everything above has to be re-created by the upscaling. The reference planes themselves carry no energy above k = 128 (their spectrum falls by more than ten orders of magnitude there, as when a spectral simulation keeps only the lower two thirds of its modes), so the scales to be re-created are k = 49 to 128, and energy that a method puts above k = 128 is spurious.

B. Temporal evolution against persistence¶

The pairs and their split are rebuilt as in Stage 2: for every plane index z the snapshots t and t + 1 that both exist form a pair, and the first 85 % of the pairs of each plane (in time) are training, the rest validation; the validation pairs are the 857 Stage 3 validates on. The normalisation is Stage 2's (mean 0.78471, standard deviation 0.34105). Persistence predicts u(t + k) = u(t) for every horizon k. A second reference, the mean profile, predicts the time- and streamwise-averaged velocity U(y) of the training period (the 50 complete planes, snapshots 30000 to 30089) at every point and every horizon: it knows the mean flow and nothing about the current field. The correlation between the fluctuation u − U(y) at the start and at each later snapshot shows how fast the flow forgets its initial state, and the simulation time stored in every file (attribute time) gives the spacing between snapshots.

Rollouts start only on the 50 planes (z = 0 to 49) that hold every snapshot from 30000 to 30106; the ten planes z = 80 to 89 have gaps and are left out.

  • Clean rollout: from the first validation snapshot of each of the 50 planes (t = 30090) to the end of the data (30106), 16 steps; every target lies in the validation period.
  • Long rollout: from the first snapshot (30000) to the last, 106 steps. Its targets up to 30090 are training fields, so it measures stability over a long horizon, not accuracy on unseen data.

    cur = {k_: X0.copy() for k_ in models}
    for h in range(1, t1 - t0 + 1):
        Y = np.stack([field(z, t0 + h) for z in full])
        for name, m in models.items():
            cur[name] = step(m, cur[name]); e = (cur[name] - Y)[:, :NY]
            with np.errstate(over="ignore", invalid="ignore"):
                mae_p = np.abs(e).mean(axis=(1, 2)); std_ratio = cur[name][:, :NY].std(axis=(1, 2)) / Y[:, :NY].std(axis=(1, 2))
            out += [dict(rollout=f"{t0}-{t1}", model=name, step=h, plane=z, mae=float(a), std_ratio=float(r)) for z, a, r in zip(full, mae_p, std_ratio)]
        e = (X0 - Y)[:, :NY]
        out += [dict(rollout=f"{t0}-{t1}", model="persistence", step=h, plane=z, mae=float(a), std_ratio=float(r))
                for z, a, r in zip(full, np.abs(e).mean(axis=(1, 2)), X0[:, :NY].std(axis=(1, 2)) / Y[:, :NY].std(axis=(1, 2)))]
        em = (MEANF[None] - Y)[:, :NY]
        out += [dict(rollout=f"{t0}-{t1}", model="mean profile", step=h, plane=z, mae=float(a), std_ratio=0.0) for z, a in zip(full, np.abs(em).mean(axis=(1, 2)))]
        f0, f1 = (X0 - MEANF[None])[:, :NY], (Y - MEANF[None])[:, :NY]
        corr = (f0 * f1).mean(axis=(1, 2)) / np.sqrt((f0 ** 2).mean(axis=(1, 2)) * (f1 ** 2).mean(axis=(1, 2)))
        out += [dict(rollout=f"{t0}-{t1}", model="correlation with the start field", step=h, plane=z, mae=np.nan, std_ratio=np.nan, corr=float(c)) for z, c in zip(full, corr)]
    return pd.DataFrame(out)
